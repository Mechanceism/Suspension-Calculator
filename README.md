# Suspension-Calculator
Calculates optimal suspension geometry based off of supplied information.

- Project plan and conventions: [GOALS.md](GOALS.md)
- Math specification: [docs/MATH.md](docs/MATH.md)

## Running the visualizer

Requires [Node.js](https://nodejs.org/) 20+.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # then open http://localhost:5173
```

For the fastest optimizer runs and smoothest playback, use the production build (`npm run build`, then `npm run preview`): React's development checks take a large share of each frame in `npm run dev`.

- **Click to edit.** Click any part in the viewport (arm, upright, tie rod, rack, coilover, wheel, chassis) to open it in the **Inspector** (left): its settings, dimensions (e.g. arm length, pivot-axis angles and the **arm's front-view angle at static height** — editing them moves the frame pivots, not the upright), its points, and chips for its connected (1st) and next (2nd) family parts.
- **Joints.** Click a joint to select it: small X / Y / Z arrows appear on it in the viewport — **drag an arrow** to move the joint along that axis (*Snap drag to grid*, 0.1–10 mm, keeps values round). The Inspector shows the position to type X/Y/Z, nudge ± in steps, or move by an amount. Values accept units (`5in`, `1ft 2in`, `250 + 3in`).
- **Move along a line.** In a joint's Inspector, *Along the line toward* slides it on its current line to another joint, e.g. the LCA rear bushing toward the lower ball joint, or along the pivot axis. Type the distance to that joint, nudge ±, or drag the yellow → arrow in the viewport; X, Y and Z change together so the direction is kept.
- **Move several at once.** <kbd>Shift</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + click joints or parts to build a multi-selection; a selected **part** also gets arrows at its center. Dragging (or *Move together* / *Move whole part* in the Inspector) moves every point by the same amount — shared joints such as ball joints and the coilover mount move with their part.
- **Measure** (`📏` button or <kbd>M</kbd>): click two points (hardpoints, contact point, patch centroid, steering-axis ground point, roll center, IC) for distance, ΔX/ΔY/ΔZ and angle — it updates live as the suspension moves. <kbd>Esc</kbd> exits.
- **Set dimension**: with two points measured, the panel shows their distance, ΔX/ΔY/ΔZ and the front-, side- and top-view angles and slope, at ride height (or at the current travel). Type a value to move one point (choose which; the other stays). Locked coordinates are respected. 📏 on a tie rod or coilover (Inspector) measures that part; *Measure / set dimension from here* on a joint starts from it. **✕ Close** (or *Cancel* on the banner, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>) leaves the tool.
- **Held dimensions** (like CAD sketch dimensions): *When set* → **Once** (just move), **Hold at ride height / at this travel** (met at that position, e.g. the LCA angle at ride height; it still moves through travel and comes back), or **Permanent** (a part dimension that never changes — offered only for a distance on one rigid part, e.g. ball joint to frame mount, or any value between chassis points). 🔓 next to a value holds it as it is. Held dimensions are drawn in the view (amber; red if they can't be met), listed in the **Locks** tab, saved with the project, kept when you edit other points (the other points in held dimensions move as little as possible; the edit you made stands) and kept by both optimizers.
- **Units** menu (top bar): **Metric**, **Imperial**, or **Custom** (length mm/cm/m/in/ft/yd, mass g/kg/oz/lb, force N/kN/lbf/kgf, pressure kPa/bar/psi, torque, temperature, angle, altitude, area). Every value and readout follows it; rates and gains follow their parts (N/mm → lbf/in, °/mm → °/in). Plain typed numbers are read in the chosen unit; a typed unit always wins. The math stays in mm/N/kg. Saved in this browser.
- **Undo / redo** every design edit: ↶ ↷ in the top bar, <kbd>Ctrl+Z</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl+Y</kbd> (a drag or a quick burst of edits is one step).
- **Help**: <kbd>?</kbd> or the **?** button lists the keyboard and mouse controls.
- **View** menu (top bar): show/hide parts and construction geometry in **every** viewport (Corner and Vehicle); one **Labels** checkbox names points and markers, each label only while its item is shown (CG (sprung) with the CG, a roll center's with the roll centers, a point's with a part it belongs to; the force arrows' numbers are their own *Show values*); motion trails, ghosts at the travel limits, camera follow, and *Plain cylinder tire* (hides the shoulder radius in the drawing; the math still uses it). ✎ selects a part for editing. **Inputs** tab: every input in one list.
- **Fit**: *⤢ Fit* (top right of the view) or **double middle-click** in a viewport fits everything in view.
- **Resize panels**: drag the edge between a side panel and the viewport (double-click the edge to reset). Sizes reset on reload.
- **Viewport**: drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Buttons lock to Front / Rear / Outboard / Inboard / Top / Bottom / Iso and switch orthographic ↔ perspective. Click empty space to deselect.
- **Motion bar** (bottom): **Travel** and **Load** sliders are both shown and linked through the spring — drag travel and the load slider shows the load that position implies; drag load and the corner settles on its spring while the travel slider follows (the one you touched is marked *input*). Quick buttons: travel *Droop / 0 / Bump*, load *0× / 1× / 2×*, steering *◀ Lock / 0 / Lock ▶*. **Steering wheel** slider (rack travel from the c-factor). Separate **▶ Travel/Load** and **▶ Steer** play buttons can run together.
- **Locks** 🔓/🔒: next to values, joint coordinates, points (all three axes) and parts (*Lock part*), and derived dimensions such as arm length. Locked values are what the optimizer will have to work around — lock what you've already built or bought. Locked inputs have an amber edge, locked joints are drawn yellow, and the drag arrows / nudges of a locked axis are disabled (typing still works). The **Locks** tab (and the Locks section in the Vehicle view) lists everything locked with its current value; click an item to jump to it — it's selected in the viewport and its row flashes in the Inspector — or ✕ to unlock.
- **Optimize** tab (Corner): pick goals (bump steer, camber gain, outside-wheel inclination in roll, roll-center height and migration, KPI, caster, scrub, trail, anti angle, motion ratio, **contact area** and **grip** of the tire, and three **limits** (≤ target, only the excess counts): **parking effort** and **effort at speed** (steering-wheel torque for both front wheels, at standstill and at the lateral g set by *Effort check at* in the Steering panel's tire forces; set the pneumatic trail there for a realistic at-speed number) and a **scrub radius limit**; the same three are vehicle-optimizer goals for every steered design, so *Optimize until it passes* / *Improve* in the Tests keep the steering reasonable), each with a target (or *max*) and weight, and the **free coordinates**, each with a min/max range (*Pick coordinates…*, with the chosen list below it; locked ones show 🔒 and are skipped, locked arm dimensions are held). The **Now** column shows the current value. Presets: **Bump steer → tie-rod mounts**, **Grip → arm geometry**. *Tire grip model* sets the friction model (μ, reference pressure, load sensitivity, carcass share).
- **Optimize** tab (Vehicle): the same for every corner design at once, judged by the whole-car solve in **load cases** (cornering, braking, accelerating g, **braking in a turn**, each with a **weight** in the averaged goals): total grip and contact area of all four tires, the **cornering limit** (the lateral g where the front or rear axle runs out of grip; it says which goes first), outside-tire inclination, roll gradient, front share of load transfer, brake pitch, anti-dive / anti-squat, bump steer. Presets: **Grip → arm geometry**, **Load-transfer split → roll centers**. A run takes seconds to a minute. *▶ Optimize* runs in the background (*Stop* keeps the best so far); the result shows each goal before → after and every coordinate's move (⚠ *limit* = it hit its range). **Apply to design** (then *Undo*) or *Discard*. Optimizer setups are saved with the project.
- **Corner / Vehicle** switch (top). *Corner* is the single-corner workbench; the **Corner** box at the top of its sidebar picks which corner you edit (FL/FR/RL/RR), its settings (steers, powered) and the dimension links (front L/R same, rear L/R same, rear same as front). The corner load follows the vehicle CG by default (Inspector → Chassis can switch it to manual).
- **Steering mechanism** (Steering → *Mechanism*): **rack and pinion**, or a **steering arm / plate** turning on a pivot axis (an ATV plate on the steering column, a steering box's pitman arm): the input becomes the arm angle (°) and the readout gives the arm torque. *Same at full lock* sets a **variable ratio** (variable-ratio rack or geared cam), or enter a **measured ratio curve** (steering-wheel angle vs input pairs). A third mechanism, **pitman arm, idler arm and center link**, carries the inner tie-rod joints on a center link moved by the steering box's pitman arm (input = its angle).
- **Steering layout:** *Rack position* ahead of or behind the axle (the steering arm is mirrored through the steering axis, so the Ackermann line stays), *Rack length* (inner ball joint to ball joint, lockable) with the tie-rod length, and **Ackermann lines** in the Vehicle view (View menu): for every corner with steering on, from its steering axis through its steering arm's outer joint, per axle. With 100 % Ackermann they cross at the other axle's center (midway when both axles steer); the readout shows how far from it they cross. **Drag a line's handle or the crossing** to turn the steering arms (the outer tie-rod joints move, keeping the arm length). **Click the crossing** for X / Y / Z arrows: Y raises or lowers both outer tie-rod joints (the arms turn about their inclined steering axes so the crossing stays put in top view), Z moves it forward / back. Only moves that would make a mirrored left / right pair differ are blocked (its crossing can't move sideways: the X arrow shows locked and says why); a notice says which linked corners move too.
- **Vehicle view editing:** click any corner's joint to select it (that corner becomes active) and drag the X / Y / Z arrows; corners sharing its dimensions move with it, mirrored, with a notice. **📏 Measure** works here too, between any joints, contact patches, the CG and the roll centers, across corners (track, wheelbase, …). View menu: CG, anti-roll bar links and marker labels (CG, roll centers, Ackermann target and crossing, anti-roll bar links) follow *Labels* and their items; *Labels* also names the active corner's ball joints (with their arms or the upright shown).
- **Spring layout** (Spring & load → *Layout*): a coilover, or **pushrod / pullrod + rocker**. The rocker pivots on the frame, the pushrod runs from the lower arm, upper arm or knuckle, and the spring sits on the rocker; the motion ratio comes from the linkage. Switching builds a working layout from the coilover (levers square to their links); move the rocker points to shape it.
- **Coilover / strut placement** (click the coilover): installed length, front- and side-view angle from vertical, and the lower mount's position along its arm (%). The values are typed and the mounts move for you. A double wishbone's coilover can also mount on the knuckle.
- **More results:** caster and KPI gain, roll camber (outside wheel's camber to the ground per degree of roll), track change (scrub) and roll-center migration per mm of travel; KPI and mechanical trail plots; **Ackermann %** (here and at full lock, and plotted against the steering wheel); in the Vehicle view, **roll stiffness** per axle (N·m/°), its front share and the **roll gradient** (°/g) while cornering.
- **Suspension types** per corner design: **double wishbone**, **MacPherson strut** (lower arm + strut from the upright to a top mount; the strut is the spring / damper), **multilink** (five links with their own upright ends), **solid axle** (one axle carries both wheels: 4-link, 3-link + Panhard rod, or **leaf springs** (front eye, seat, rear shackle; the leaf locates and springs the axle), or 3-link + **Watt's linkage**; optionally **steered** (knuckles on kingpins at the axle ends, a cross tie rod and a drag link; the leaf-spring axle too; ticking *Steers* on a solid-axle corner turns it on; a twist beam doesn't steer: choosing one turns steering off on its corners, and a type that steers offers it back); presets *A4*, *A3*, *AW*, *AS* (steered), *LF*; the roll center, side-view IC and roll steer come from its links), **twist beam** (trailing arms joined by a twisting beam: trailing arms in heave, each arm turns about bushing → shear center in roll; the beam's twist is the axle's anti-roll bar; preset *TB*; a solid axle or twist beam is one part under both wheels, so its left and right always share one design and the Vehicle view draws it once), **single arm** (the knuckle rides on one arm and steers on a kingpin carried by it: swing arm / long-travel trophy-truck style, or trailing arm; presets *S*, *T*) **no suspension** (rigid, e.g. a kart: the wheel steers on a fixed kingpin, only the tire gives), and a **custom linkage** (your own bodies joined by ball joints, hinges / bushings, rods, slide-and-turn and slider joints to the frame, the steering rack and the knuckle, in the *Linkage* editor; it counts the free motions and says what's missing, and its instant axis and steering axis come from how the knuckle actually moves; click a joint's point to drag it, and the corner optimizer can move them). Choose in the Inspector (with nothing selected) or Inputs → *Type*, in the setup wizard, or start from presets *M* / *L*. Switching keeps the lower arm, tie rod, hub and tire and places the new parts from them; a multilink starts out moving like the wishbones it came from. The steering axis is the ball joints, the lower ball joint to the strut top, or the multilink's virtual pivots; instant centers, roll center and every result follow. Mixed types work across the whole car, tests and optimizers.
- **Vehicle** view: the whole car settles on its springs and tires. Inputs: mass, CG, wheelbase, brake bias, drive split, per-corner preload fitting, and **shared components per axle** (anti-roll bar: none / rate at wheel / torsion bar + drop links on the lower or upper arm, the knuckle or the rocker (on a solid axle or twist beam: on the axle or the arms); heave spring: none / rate at wheel). Each corner's **brake** (outboard / inboard / none) and, for powered corners, **drive** (chassis diff + halfshaft / hub motor) is in the Corner box. Lateral acceleration, longitudinal (braking ← → acceleration) and steering sliders with Play. The **Forces** box in the view draws the forces as arrows where they act — tire vertical / lateral / brake-drive, springs, anti-roll bar / heave, weights, inertia — with optional values and a size slider. Results: corner loads, travel, inclination, roll / pitch / heave, ride heights, roll centers and roll axis, load transfer through links vs. springs, LLTD, rollover threshold. Double-click a corner to edit it.
- **Corner box** (Corner and Vehicle views): per corner *Steers*, *Powered* (rear wheels by default) (drive: chassis diff + halfshaft, with an **Open / Limited-slip / Locked** differential, or hub motor) and *Brake* (outboard / inboard / none) with **ABS** (on by default).
- **Dampers**: bump and rebound damping rates per corner (Inspector → Coilover), the **damping ratio** in the results (0.25–0.4 ride, 0.6–0.7 handling) and *Set damper rates* for a target ratio. Dampers act only in motion, so the steady-state views don't use them yet; the time-based tests (Phase B) will.
- **Towing** (Vehicle → Inputs → *Towing*): trailer mass, tongue weight, hitch height and position, hitch to the trailer axle, trailer CG height and trailer brakes. The trailer's tongue load, push / pull and side force act on the car at the hitch (shown in the results), in the steady states, the ride and handling over time. With the driver steering (Handling), the trailer is its own body and can **sway**: set its yaw radius of gyration; a *Trailer sway* pass rule fails above ±5° of swing or on a jackknife, and playback shows its articulation and sway (with the trailer on the track map). Less tongue weight, a heavier or longer-bodied trailer, or more speed makes it sway sooner.
- **Brakes, more (round 3):** per axle **Disc / Drum** (drum: wheel-cylinder bore, drum radius, shoe brake factor; drums fade sooner), the **largest rotor / drum that fits the wheel** (from the rim and the room the caliper needs), **Hydraulics: Simple / Detailed** (detailed: master-cylinder stroke, most pedal travel, pad / shoe clearance and the circuits' swell, giving the pedal travel and the stroke used; tests flag **Long pedal** and **Master cylinder runs out**), **what would fix it** when the last test runs found the brakes too weak (each change that would be enough, and whether it fits), and the **brake ladder** (Tests → Optimize → *Brakes (step ladder)*): the smallest standard calipers and rotors that keep a reserve in the hardest stop (more pistons first, larger rotors last, stepping down when there is a lot to spare, holding the hydraulic bias); drums switch to discs only when you allow it.
- **Brake system** (Vehicle → Inputs → *Brake system*): off (the brakes are always strong enough) or **the hydraulics**: pedal force and ratio, booster, one tandem master cylinder or two on a balance bar, caliper pistons, pads and rotors. You get line pressure, brake torque, the most braking force per axle and in g, and the hydraulic bias (optionally used as the brake bias). The pedal force a braking case needs is shown in the results, and a stop the brakes can't make fails **Brakes too weak** in the tests. **Brake fade** (optional, with the hydraulics): rotor mass, cooling, the pads' fade temperatures and grip loss; the rotors heat with every stop through a test (temperatures per segment and in playback), and hot pads lose grip: a *Brake fade* warning, then *Brakes too weak* when the faded brakes can't make the stop.
- **Aerodynamics** (Vehicle → Inputs → *Aerodynamics*): *Off (negligible)* by default; *Generic values* (drag area CdA, downforce area front / rear, drag height, altitude and air temperature) or **Import aero data** (also File → Import → *Aero data*): constant coefficients or an **aero map** over front / rear ride height, as JSON or CSV, e.g. exported from a CFD tool (format in [docs/MATH.md](docs/MATH.md#aerodynamics--srccorevehicleaerots)). Drag acts on the body (the driven tires push against it), downforce at each axle (loads the tires, pitches the car). A *Speed* slider in the motion bar (when aero is on) and the **Aero** results (drag, downforce front / rear, aero balance); tests use each moment's speed.
- **Vehicle view: road and grip.** *Grade* (%), *Bank* (°) and a **bump / pothole under each wheel** sit in the motion bar. The **Grip (tires)** results show each tire's grip use (combined cornering and braking/drive force ÷ what it can carry), its state (grip, sliding, ABS limit, locked, wheelspin) and what limits the car (understeer / oversteer, braking, traction). Front/rear cornering force follows the CG position (yaw balance); within an axle both tires use the same share of their grip.
- **Tests** workspace (top bar: Corner · Vehicle · **Tests**): a test is a chain of **segments**, each combining **speed** (start → end over a distance, or a g override), **path** (straight / left / right with a radius, or a lateral-g override) and **road** (grade, bank, a seeded **rough surface** — seed 🎲, max height, scale, coverage, with a preview of the height map, wheel paths and the profile under each wheel — and placed **bumps / potholes** with position, length, height and side). The worst road spots (highest / lowest wheel, most twist) are checked as extra states. Tests and their segments are plain numbered lists (drag or ↑/↓ to reorder, ⧉ duplicate, ✕ remove; click one to edit it). Each test has an **enable** checkbox (untick to skip it), ▶ to run just that test, and a ✓ / ⚠ / ✗ status from its last run; **▶▶ Run all** runs every enabled test in order. *+ New test* starts an empty test; **Presets** (**autocross (simple)**; **autocross (complex)**: flat, a fast slalom (≈ 0.7 g), two mirrored sets of offset gates and a broad 90° sweeper each way, with the same number and kind of left and right turns; pothole while braking in a turn, hills, **oval with banked turns** (short NASCAR-style track: 24° banked 180° turns at 75 mph, banked straights), **balanced** (equal left and right turns, each braked into and accelerated out of, a small hill: loads every corner evenly)) start from an example; grade and bank are resolved exactly in the road plane, so a banked turn needs less side grip and presses the car into the track; *Preset:* next to *+ Add segment* picks what to add (Empty, 0–60, brake 60–0, turns, incline / decline, speed bump, pothole, rough road). A **track map** above the segments draws the test from its segments (click a segment on it to edit it; colored by the last run; bumps / potholes marked, rough road dashed). Tests have a **priority** A, B, C… and segments P1, P2… (most important first; equal = same priority), separate from the order: when optimizing, a rank counts n + 1 − rank (two tests A / B → 2 : 1). **Steering**: each turn needs the road-wheel angle atan(wheelbase ÷ radius) plus the front − rear tire slip angle; the rack travel for it comes from the car's steering kinematics, the wheels steer in playback, and a turn tighter than the rack limit fails (*Steering lock*). **Pass rules**: every check (near the grip limit, understeer, oversteer / drift, wheelspin, ABS active, lock-up, speed not reached, bump-stop contact, bottoming, wheel lift, steering lock, rollover, beyond the tires) is Pass / **Warning** / Fail with thresholds; profiles (Default, Street, Track, Drift, Off-road, and your own via *Save as profile…*) per test, overridable per segment. *▶ Run test* checks every segment at several speeds: **Passed / Passed with warnings / Failed**, the findings and the **speed that would pass**. Velocity changes come only from the tires: speeding up needs powered wheels, slowing down needs brakes. A segment asking for more than the tires can give is flagged as impossible for any suspension. *Driver lifts* re-runs failing segments at that speed; *Apply speed limit* writes it into the segment; **Optimize** (in the results), for *This test* or *All enabled tests*: *▶ Optimize until it passes* runs the vehicle optimizer with the tests as its load cases (your other selected goals stay on), applies the result, re-runs the tests and repeats until they pass; when it stops making progress it widens the free ranges step by step (×2, ×3, ×4, ×5, ×10…) up to **Max widening** (my ranges only … no limit) and keeps going, and explains what blocks it when it is stuck or the failures can't be fixed by geometry; *▶ Improve* (when it passes) lowers grip use everywhere, priority-weighted; *Preview wider ranges* tries once with the ranges at the maximum widening, tests the candidate without applying it and lets you accept it (widening those ranges) or keep your ranges. While anything runs, a spinner with **Stop** shows in the top bar. Every applied round is one undo step. In the vehicle optimizer the load cases can be one test or **all tests by priority**; *Free all available* frees every unlocked coordinate. Free points can't move into the tire or below the ground. Clicking a segment shows the car in that state; *Show in Vehicle view* carries it over. **Playback** (under the 3D view): *▶ Play* (or **Space**) drives the car through the test every 0.1 s (play / pause, scrub, ¼×–2×), with a top-view track map colored Pass / Warning / Fail and the speed, g's and each tire's grip use as it goes (over time when the test has **Ride over time** on, see below; otherwise state to state). Speeds show in km/h with Metric and mph with Imperial (Units menu; Custom lets you mix). Grip uses the Magic Formula tire model.
- **Ride over time (Phase B)**, per test (*Ride over time: dynamics*, on by default): *Run test* also drives the test over time as a 7-degree-of-freedom car (body heave, roll and pitch plus each wheel bouncing on its tire), with the springs, bump stops, anti-roll bars, the **bump / rebound damper rates** (corner Damper inputs), tire stiffness and damping, the road under each wheel at the car's speed, and smooth 0.3 s changes of the accelerations at segment boundaries (transients). Every 10 ms is checked by the pass rules: wheel lift, bump-stop contact and bottoming count at once, grip problems when they last at least 0.1 s; each shows once per segment with when it first happened and how often (e.g. *FL lifts off the ground (at 6.5 s, 6 times)*). A segment that passes as steady states but fails over time says so. **Playback** then shows the real body motion (roll and pitch in the readout) and starts at once (it reuses the run). Vehicle inputs: *Body roll / pitch inertia* (about the sprung CG) and *Tire damping*.
- **Shimmy / death wobble** (Vehicle → Inputs → *Shimmy / death wobble*): steering-system stiffness and damper, steering-wheel inertia, hands on / off, and worn parts (free play, stiffness and damping left, wheel imbalance). Live: new and worn, whether the front wheels' oscillation (4–25 Hz) dies out up to the fastest speed (onset speed, least damping) and whether a kick at that speed settles; a steered solid axle includes its tramp (death wobble). Goal **Shimmy margin** in the vehicle optimizer (checked again with the worn kick test before the result is offered), and a **Shimmy** check in every test.
- **Tire choice** (Tests → Optimize → *Tires (choose a size)*): tick sizes from a catalog (road, race / FSAE, off-road) or type your own, pick the axle, the room around the tire and whether rims may change, and rank them on the tests (optionally re-optimizing the geometry for each). A loaded .tir is approximated at each size (nominal load, radius, width scaled; marked, with a warning far from the measured size); the tire + wheel's estimated weight changes the unsprung mass; *Use* puts the chosen tire on the car.
- **Speed from forces** (with *the driver works the pedals*): the car's speed comes from the forces: the tires' drive / braking (what the driver asks, within grip and the pedals the car has), drag, grade, **rolling resistance** (Vehicle → *Rolling resistance*, default 0.012) and **the bumps' push** (a tire climbing a bump is pushed back; the energy it takes is what the dampers turn into heat). A segment can **Coast** (no throttle or brakes). New check **Kickback**: a jolt at the steering wheel (bumps and braking through the scrub radius and kingpin offset; Warning above 5 N·m).
- **Handling (Phase C)**, per test (*Handling: steered by a driver*, on by default with the ride): over time a driver steers the car along the path, through the car's own steering kinematics (Ackermann from the geometry) with a steering lag, a rate limit and the rack limit, and each tire's side force comes from its **slip angle** (Magic Formula at its load, camber and pressure, sharing grip with braking / drive). The car yaws and can slide: too fast for the front grip it goes to full lock and runs wide; too little rear grip and it spins. New pass rules: **Off the line** (Fail beyond 1 m) and **Spin / slide** (sideslip, Fail beyond 12°); a tire past its peak slip angle counts as sliding. **Playback** places the car on the line it actually drove (track map: driven line over the grey path) with the distance off the line, sideslip and steer angle in the readout. Vehicle input: *Yaw inertia* (whole car; the setup wizard estimates it). Off: the car follows the path exactly (as in Phase B).
- **Setup wizard** (File → Setup wizard, or Vehicle → Inputs): enter your car step by step, easy values first: suspension type and which corners share dimensions, tire sizes, pressures, **rim width** (a wider rim spreads the tire) and **wheel offset (ET)** (the wheel-center point is the hub mounting face; the tire sits ET inboard), track and wheelbase (or the overall width, with a diagram of what each is), weight and CG (directly, or with the **corner-scale calculator**: four corner weights, then one axle raised for the CG height), unsprung weight (sum of the parts, half of the parts joining the body), suspension points (opens the point table), springs (rate, or **measure it with a weight**: body drop → spring rate with the tire and motion ratio taken out), dampers (generic from damping ratios, or your rates) and anti-roll bars, **steering** (steering-wheel turns lock to lock or center to lock, and one wheel's angle full left to full right or straight to full lock → rack travel, travel per turn, inside / outside angles at full lock, steering ratio, tightest turn), **tire grip** (.tir import or fit), body inertia (estimate from weight and size). Each step: *Apply and next* or *Use an estimate*; estimates stay marked and the results say what rests on them. **What you enter is locked** so no optimizer changes it (tire size, rim, offset and pressure; track and wheelbase as the wheel centers' X and Z; weight and CG; unsprung weight; spring rates; damper rates and anti-roll bars; steering; inertias), listed in the **Locks** tab marked *setup wizard*; unlock any you want optimized. *Use an estimate* leaves a step's values free (and frees what that step had locked); locks you set yourself are never touched. Every change can be undone.
- **Uses every core:** the vehicle optimizer (and the Tests' *Optimize until it passes* / *Improve*), *What matters most*, spring / damper tuning and *Run all* spread their work over a pool of background workers, one per spare CPU core (up to 8): every design the optimizer needs to try in a step is evaluated at the same time, and several tests run at once. The 3D view already draws on the GPU; the GPU isn't used for the physics (many small, branching solves suit CPU cores better). Playback no longer rebuilds the 3D scene every frame.
- **Optimizer search:** *Search* (Corner, Vehicle and Tests optimizers): *Local* from your design, or *Wide* (4 or 8 starts from the best of a design of experiments, keeping the best result). **What matters most** (Vehicle → Optimize, and in the Tests optimize section): samples designs across the free ranges and ranks which coordinates change the result most (bars: red = moving toward the top of the range makes it worse), with the strongest interactions and what drives each goal. **Springs & dampers (ride over time)** in the Tests optimize section: tick springs / dampers / anti-roll bars, the search range (÷/× around your values) and the number of tries; *▶ Tune* runs Bayesian optimization (each try runs the tests in scope, steady and over time; your current rates are the first try, so it never ends worse) and applies the best rates (one undo step). With *also inside Optimize until it passes*, the loop tunes them when the remaining failures only show over time or when geometry stops helping.
- **Dampers:** *Linear* (one rate per direction, default) or *Two-stage* (low-speed rate up to a knee shaft speed, then a high-speed rate; generic: half the rate past 0.1 m/s), with a force-vs-shaft-speed curve (Inputs → Damper & bump stop).
- **Tire `.tir` files:** **File → Import → Tire (.tir)** (all four tires, after the usual replace prompt), or in *Tire grip model*, **Import .tir** loads a fitted Magic Formula tire property file (MF 6.1 / 6.2, or 5.2) and uses its full force model instead of the simple one (camber stiffness, shifts, load and pressure dependence, combined slip). It shows the file's nominal load and pressure, the curves and peaks, and two **grip scales** (λμy side, λμx braking / drive) to adjust for the surface; the model is saved in your project. *Simple (generic)* switches back; *Remove the file* drops it. When the file gives a tire size you're offered to use it. **Different rear tire** gives the rear its own model (Front / Rear tabs; *Same as front* joins them again). **Fit a tire model to measured data**: load a table of measurements (SA, SL, FZ, IA, P, FX, FY; CSV or tab-separated, e.g. a test-rig run exported as text), *▶ Fit*, check the error, R² and the data-vs-model plot, then *Use for all tires / Front only / Rear only* or *Download .tir*. No tire files come with the app: load your own tire's file or data.
- **Tire grip model** (Vehicle or Corner → Optimize → *Tire grip model*): the **Magic Formula** (Pacejka) gives each tire's peak side and braking / drive force from its load (load sensitivity), its lean toward the turn (camber thrust; leaning out costs grip), and its pressure, plus the sliding share past the peak and the slip angle / slip ratio it needs. Live curves of force ÷ load vs slip angle and slip ratio, and a summary (peak μ, slip at the peak, best lean, sliding share). The coefficients are **generic** until fitted to your tire; every one is editable (basic first, the rest under *Advanced*), *Reset to generic* restores them. Grip use (friction ellipse), slip angles and each tire's lean show in the Vehicle view's grip table and the test results; the steering a turn needs includes the front − rear slip angle (understeer). The contact-patch pressure model stays for the contact-area goal.
- **File** menu (top left): New (default layout), **New from car profile** (road car, FSAE car, kart, trophy truck: a whole car to start from, every value editable), Save (<kbd>Ctrl+S</kbd>), Save as (<kbd>Ctrl+Shift+S</kbd>), Recents (saved in this browser; model and tests). **Export** and **Import** in three parts: *model and tests*, *model only*, *tests only* (`.susp.json`, `.susp-tests.json`). Every import first says what it will replace and offers **Save**, **Save as**, **Overwrite** or Cancel (for settings, Save / Save as export the current settings first). **Settings** export / import: display units, view options and your pass-rule profiles (`.susp-settings.json`). Unsaved changes are tracked per part since its last save or export: the title shows **• model** / **• tests**, and New / Open / Import ask before discarding (closing the tab gets the browser's generic warning).
- **Results / Curves** (right, collapsible): geometry, spring & ride, steering (angles, ratio, axis torque, tie-rod / rack force, steering-wheel torque, parking scrub torque), tire & patch; curves through travel and through steering.

URL options for sharing a specific view: `?preset=A&view=front&travel=30&rack=10&labels=1&projection=perspective&mode=loaded&load=1.5&tire=gas`.
